# Risk Reward Ratio in Swing Trading: The Complete Guide (With Examples)

You found a clean breakout. Entry looks perfect. Then the trade stalls, you move your stop, exit at breakeven, and watch price rip to your old target without you. Most swing traders do not fail because they cannot read charts — they fail because **risk reward ratio** was never defined before the buy button was pressed.

This guide covers what R:R means, how to calculate it, ₹10k and ₹25k examples, win rate vs risk reward, and how to build the habit on every swing trade.

---

## What is Risk Reward Ratio?

**Risk reward ratio** (R:R) compares reward (entry to target) against risk (entry to stop).

- **Risk** = |Entry − Stop|
- **Reward** = |Target − Entry|
- **Ratio** = Reward ÷ Risk → expressed as **1:X**

**Example:** Entry ₹1,000, stop ₹970, target ₹1,090 → risk ₹30, reward ₹90 → **1:3 risk reward**.

---

## Why Professional Traders Never Ignore It

Professionals treat R:R as a **filter before capital is deployed**:

- Skips setups where payoff does not justify gap and slippage risk
- Pairs R:R with **position sizing** so loss streaks stay inside limits
- Reviews **average planned R vs realized R** in the journal

Retail optimizes for being right. Professionals optimize for being **paid** when right.

## Win Rate vs Risk Reward

**Expectancy** (per rupee risked) ≈ (Win% × Avg win) − (Loss% × Avg loss).

| R:R | Breakeven win rate |
| --- | --- |
| 1:1 | 50% |
| 1:2 | 33.3% |
| 1:3 | 25% |
| 1:5 | 16.7% |

At **40% win rate** and **2R average winners**, expectancy is positive. At 1:1, the same win rate loses money.

---

## 1:1 vs 1:2 vs 1:3 vs 1:5

| Ratio | Breakeven % | Best for | Trade-off |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1:1 | 50% | Tight range plays | Needs high accuracy |
| 1:2 | 33% | **Default swing baseline** | Balanced |
| 1:3 | 25% | Trend continuation | Lower hit rate |
| 1:5 | 17% | Home-run swings | Many targets missed |

---

## Why Profitable Traders Win Only ~40% of Trades

Four wins at **2.5R** = +10R. Six losses at **1R** = −6R. Net **+4R** with a 40% win rate.

Swing trading rewards **asymmetry**: small, honored losses and larger, patient winners.

## Common Beginner Mistakes

- Measuring R:R after price moves, not at entry
- Tight stops that inflate R:R on paper but get shaken out
- Fantasy targets you never intend to hold for
- Ignoring brokerage on small accounts
- Moving stops wider when wrong

- Minimum R:R rule (e.g. never below **1:2**)

- Log every trade; review losers first
- Include fees in mental math
- Revisit breakeven win rate quarterly

## Conclusion

**Risk reward ratio swing trading** is simple math with hard discipline. Define stop and target before entry, size correctly, and log results. Professionals accept sub-50% win rates when payoff is skewed.

## FAQ

1. **What is a good risk reward ratio for swing trading?** — Often 1:2 minimum; 1:3 if your edge supports lower hit rates.
2. **How do you calculate risk reward?** — Reward ÷ risk from entry to target and stop.
3. **Is 1:2 enough?** — Yes, above ~33% win rate before costs.
4. **Why win only 40%?** — Higher R on winners compensates.
5. **Same R:R every trade?** — Use a floor; allow flexibility when structure supports it.
6. **Include brokerage?** — Yes, especially on small accounts.
7. **R:R vs position sizing?** — Geometry vs quantity; need both.
8. **Trade at 1:1?** — Needs >50% wins after costs.
9. **SwingTradingLog tracking?** — Planned R from logged entry/stop/target.
10. **Best for beginners?** — Start at 1:2; use the risk calculator every time.
